# The seven fields

Fixed order. One page. The ask is first because a memo that builds to its request loses the reader who stopped after the summary, which is most of them.

## 1. The ask

One sentence. What you want approved, bounded.

> Approve launching Quick Send on mobile at a 10,000 passwordless limit, with no daily cap, in the Q4 campaign window.

If the sentence needs an "and", check whether it is two decisions. Two decisions in one memo means the reader either approves something they had a question about, or blocks the half they agreed with.

## 2. Why now

What changed, or what closes. A memo with no clock attached is filed rather than answered.

> The campaign window is fixed and the mobile release train closes on the 12th. After that the earliest slot is February.

If nothing is urgent, say so plainly and give a date you want an answer by anyway. An honest "this is not urgent, but I need a yes by the 20th to plan" is respected. A manufactured urgency is noticed and remembered.

## 3. What we know

The evidence, compressed, in the author's voice — including what is not known.

> Mobile carries a million transfers a month. Survey comments name the transfer flow as too long; we have no funnel data on where people drop out. Three competitors ship an equivalent.

**Say the gaps.** *"We have not measured this"* is a sentence a senior reader respects and can act on — often by asking for the measurement, which is a better outcome than an approval built on nothing. A memo that reads as complete and is not is the one that costs the author their next memo.

## 4. The recommendation

Your position, and why. One paragraph.

> Ship it. The volume opportunity is real, the build is small, and the exposure is bounded by a limit we can change remotely. The risk that matters is fraud on stolen devices, and the mitigation — remote kill switch and threshold — exists before launch rather than after.

Take the position. "It depends on your priorities" is the absence of advice wearing its clothes.

## 5. What it costs

Two parts, both required.

**What we give up:** the alternatives, one line each, with why they lose.

> A daily cap as well would close the main fraud path, and costs two weeks we do not have before the window. Recommended for the following release, not this one.

**What we accept:** the downside of the recommendation, stated by you rather than discovered by them.

> Until a daily cap exists, a stolen unlocked device can move up to the limit repeatedly.

A memo that names its own worst fact is trusted on everything else. One that omits it is disbelieved on everything else the moment the fact surfaces — and it always surfaces, usually in the room.

**A third part, whenever the ask is a reduction:** what it takes to undo. Cutting scope, stopping work, deferring a thing — the reader's first question is whether they can have it back, and it arrives before the question about what it saves.

> The passwordless path returns on two conditions: risk approval, and confirmation that the threshold can be enforced server-side. **No migration** — the applied threshold is stored on every transfer in this slice too, which is part of the cut rather than a precaution taken afterwards.

Say what brings it back and what it costs then. **If the honest answer is a migration rather than a decision, say so** — that turns the ask from a deferral into a permanent cut, and the reader is entitled to know which one they are approving. Where a `slice` pass exists it has already written this column; lift it rather than judging it again.

## 6. If nobody decides

The strongest field, and the one most memos leave out.

> With no decision by the 12th the release train closes and this moves to February, past the campaign. The build is already done; the cost of waiting is the window, not the work.

Say what happens **by default**, and when the default becomes irreversible. Inaction is a decision that gets made accidentally, and naming it is what turns a memo from information into a request.

## 7. What I need from you

Named person, specific act, date.

> **Ayşe — approve the 10,000 limit with no daily cap for this release, by Friday the 8th.** Risk has signed off on the kill switch; this is the one open approval.

If more than one person must sign, list each with what each is approving. A memo where two people each assume the other is deciding is a memo that dies quietly.

## What this memo does not ask for

An optional final line, and worth it whenever the decision sits near a bigger one. It stops the meeting expanding into everything adjacent.

> This does not ask for a decision on web, on daily limits, or on the fraud model beyond this feature.
