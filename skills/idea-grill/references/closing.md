# Closing the grill

The close is built from the **defense ledger** — the running record of which provocations held, cracked, or went unanswered. Write it from that record, not from your overall impression of the conversation. Impression drifts toward whoever spoke last and most confidently.

## Format

### Surviving thesis
The idea as it stands **after** the grill, in one or two sentences. This is almost never the opening thesis — it is usually narrower, more conditional, or aimed at a different problem. State it as the user would have to state it now, using their own words where they earned them.

### Cracks
Every `cracked` or `unanswered` entry, with what would close it. Be specific about the closer — "needs data" is useless; "needs the actual churn rate for accounts under 10 seats" is actionable.

| Crack | What would close it |
|---|---|
| … | … |

### Fatal flaw
Only if one exists. A defect that no defense addressed and that no reasonable amount of work removes. Do not manufacture one to look rigorous, and do not soften one to be kind.

### How the idea evolved
Two or three lines tracing what changed under pressure: what was dropped, what narrowed, what got sharper. This is often the most useful part of the output — it shows the user their own reasoning moving.

### Verdict
One of the four below, with a single sentence of justification.

## Verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| **SURVIVES** | Held under pressure. Remaining cracks are minor and addressable. | Proceed. Shape it into a request. |
| **SURVIVES, NARROWED** | Holds only in a reduced scope, for a narrower audience, or under conditions that were not in the original claim. The most common honest outcome. | Proceed with the narrowed version explicitly written down — not the original one. |
| **UNRESOLVED** | Key defenses went unanswered. The idea is neither dead nor defensible yet. | Name what evidence or decision is missing, then re-grill once it exists. |
| **FATAL FLAW** | A defect surfaced that no defense addressed. | Stop. Kill it here, where it is cheap. |

## Rules

- **Never inflate the verdict to be encouraging.** "SURVIVES" for an idea with three unanswered defenses is a lie that costs the user months.
- **Never manufacture a fatal flaw to look rigorous.** If it held, say it held, and say which attacks it survived — that is the strongest thing you can hand someone going into a funding conversation.
- **Quote the user.** Their own strongest sentence is more persuasive to them than your paraphrase.
- **Then offer to save.** "Want me to save this to a file?" — and only write it if they say yes.
